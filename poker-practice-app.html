Practice hands

What's the best way to practice poker without risking money?

Practice single decisions with feedback, not whole play-money games. Take a real spot, choose fold, call or raise, and get told the better play and why. Do it a few hundred times with the outcome hidden, so you judge the decision and not the luck. That is the fastest way anyone has found to build instincts you can trust when real chips are on the line.

Why play-money tables do not work

When nothing is at stake, nobody folds. Every pot goes five ways to the river, and the hand that wins is usually the one that got lucky. Play a thousand of those hands and you learn one lesson: everything is worth a call. That is the exact habit that empties your stack in a real game.

Play-money is fine for learning the mechanics of a hand. It is a poor way to learn when to fold, which is the skill that pays.

What a practice hand looks like in PowerFold Theory

The Table deals you one spot at a time. Here is roughly how one goes.

The spot

You hold A♠ J♦ on the button. A player in middle position raises to three big blinds, the next two fold. Your stack is 80 big blinds.

Your decision

Fold, call or raise. You pick before anything else happens.

The feedback

The better play, the reasoning in plain English (position, your hand against a reasonable opening range, what a raise does to the rest of the hand), a note on what the other choices cost you, and a pointer to the lesson behind it if you missed. What you never see is the flop, turn or river. The outcome stays hidden on purpose.

Why the outcome is hidden

Results lie. A good fold that would have made a flush is still a good fold. A bad call that spiked two pair is still a bad call. The moment you see the river, you start grading your decisions by whether they won, and poker punishes that way of thinking slowly and expensively. PowerFold hides the outcome so the only thing you can judge is the thing you control.

How a session works

  • Ten hands make a session. About five minutes.
  • You keep a streak. Correct decisions in a row.
  • Four levels. Level 1 keeps you preflop. Higher levels deal flops, turns and rivers as your accuracy climbs, so the spots get harder when you are ready and not before.
  • Every miss points to a lesson. A preflop mistake sends you to Starting Hands. A pot-odds mistake sends you to Outs and Odds.
  • Beginner or Advanced coaching. Beginner hides the hand-strength meter so you learn to read the spot yourself. Advanced shows it and goes deeper in the feedback.
