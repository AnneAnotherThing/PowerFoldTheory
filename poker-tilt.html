Tilt

How do I stop tilting in poker?

Tilt is deciding from feeling instead of from the spot. You stop it with three habits: judge every decision by whether it was right, not whether it won; decide before you sit how much you can lose and leave when you hit it; and build a simple decision routine you run on every hand, so you have something to lean on when you are angry. Everyone tilts. Winners just don't pay for it!

The cards you're dealt matter far less than what you do when the pressure rises and your stack is on the line. The PowerFold Principle

What tilt really is

Anger after a bad beat is the famous version, but it is only one. Tilt is anything that moves your decision away from the spot in front of you:

  • Revenge. Calling the player who just stacked you with a hand you would fold against anyone else.
  • Feeling due. Chasing because you have missed six draws and "one has to hit."
  • Sunk cost. Refusing to fold because you already put a third of your stack in.
  • Fear. Folding good hands after a loss because you cannot take another one.
  • Boredom. Playing junk because nothing has come in an hour.

All five have the same shape. The spot says one thing, the feeling says another, and the feeling wins.

Why outcomes cause it

Poker pays out on results, so your brain learns to grade decisions by results. That is the trap. In the short run the right play loses all the time, and the wrong play gets paid. If you let the river tell you whether you played well, every session becomes a mood swing, and a mood is not a strategy.

The fix is to grade yourself on the decision, which is the only thing you control. A good fold that would have made a flush is still a good fold.

Three habits that stop tilt

1. Judge the decision, not the result

After any big hand, ask one question: given what I knew, was that the right play? If yes, the money is already on its way back to you over time. If no, there is a lesson, and the lesson is the same whether you won or lost. This is a habit, and habits are built with repetition, not with reading about them.

2. Set the stop-loss before you sit

Decide the most you will lose tonight before the first card. Write it down if you have to. When you hit it, you stand up, and the decision was made by a calm version of you, not the one who just lost a flipped coin. The same rule works in reverse: a bankroll plan tells you what stakes you can afford so that one bad night is a bad night and not a crisis.

3. Run the same routine on every hand

Position. Hand. Pot. Price. Four words, every single hand, including the boring ones. When you are steaming, you will not think your way to a good decision from scratch. You will reach for whatever habit is there. Make sure it is this one.

Questions people ask

What is tilt in poker?
Tilt is making decisions from emotion instead of from the spot in front of you. Anger after a bad beat is the famous version, but revenge calls, chasing because you feel due, and refusing to fold because you already put money in are all tilt.
How do I stop tilting in poker?
Three habits. Judge every decision by whether it was right, not by whether it won. Decide before you sit how much you can lose and leave when you hit it. Build a simple decision routine (position, hand, pot, price) that you run on every hand, so you have something to lean on when you are angry.
Can you practice not tilting?
Yes, by practicing decisions where you never see the outcome. When you cannot see whether a hand would have won, you stop grading yourself on luck and start grading yourself on the choice. That habit carries to the real table.
Is tilt only about anger?
No. Fear is tilt too: folding good hands after a loss because you cannot take another one. So is boredom, playing junk because nothing has come in an hour. Anything that moves your decision away from the spot is tilt.
