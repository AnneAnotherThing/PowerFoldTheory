Sharpen your game

How do I get better at poker?

Find your single biggest leak, fix only that for two weeks, and judge yourself on the decision, not on whether the hand won. Most players who know the rules lose for one of four reasons, not twenty. Fix the one that is costing you the most and your results move before anything else changes!

Why knowing the rules is not enough

You have played for years. You know what beats what, you have read a book, you have watched the pros on television. And the same two friends still take your money every month. That is not bad luck and it is not a lack of knowledge. It is a habit you cannot see, repeated a few hundred times a night.

Experience at the table does not fix habits, because the table never tells you which decision was wrong. It only tells you whether the hand won, and the two are not the same thing. Players repeat the same leak for a decade and call it their style.

The four leaks that cost home-game players the most

1. Too many hands before the flop

The most common leak by a mile. Suited junk from early position, any ace, any two paint cards. Each one is a small, slow loss, and there are forty of them a night. The fix is a tight, boring set of starting hands per seat and the discipline to fold everything else. Boring wins.

2. Calling when the price is wrong

You have a flush draw, the pot is small, and the bet is big. You call because "I might hit." Pot odds decide that call, not hope. Count your outs, compare them with what the pot is paying, and the decision makes itself. Most players never learn to do this in their head, and it costs them every draw they ever chase.

3. Ignoring position

The same hand is a fold from the first seat and a raise from the button, because acting last means you see everyone else's decision before you make yours. Players who play every hand the same way from every seat are giving away the biggest structural edge in the game.

4. Tilt

One bad beat and the next hour is played on feeling. Revenge calls, chasing because you are "due", refusing to fold because you already put money in. Tilt erases a whole night of good decisions in twenty minutes. How to stop tilting has the full fix.

How to find yours

Stop reviewing sessions and start reviewing decisions. After a night, you remember the hands that won or lost big. Those are results, and they tell you almost nothing. What you need is a few hundred individual spots where someone tells you, each time, whether the choice was right and why. Patterns show up fast. If you miss the same kind of spot three times in a row, that is your leak.

A routine that works in 15 minutes a day

Every day, in this order
  1. Ten practice hands with the outcome hidden, so you grade the decision and not the luck.
  2. Read the one lesson your misses point to. Not the whole library. The one.
  3. Ask one question about the spot you were least sure of. Out loud, to a coach, until the answer is boring.
  4. Next week, move the level up so the spots get harder only once the easy ones are automatic.

Two weeks of that fixes a leak. A few hundred decisions builds an instinct. Years at the table without feedback does neither.

When to study GTO

Later. Solver output assumes you already think in ranges and frequencies. If you are still calling flush draws at the wrong price, a solver will not help, and it will make you feel like you are studying. Position, hand selection, pot odds and tilt control are worth more to every home-game and low-stakes player than any chart. Once those are automatic, by all means go deeper.

Questions people ask

I know the rules. Why am I still losing?
Almost always one of four leaks: playing too many hands before the flop, calling when the pot is not offering the right price, ignoring position, or tilting after a loss. Knowing the rules does not fix any of them. Repetition with feedback does.
What is the fastest way to improve at poker?
Find your single biggest leak and fix only that for two weeks. Practice real decisions with feedback every day, ten hands at a time, and judge yourself on the decision rather than on whether the hand won. One fixed leak is worth more than ten half-learned concepts.
Should I study GTO to get better?
Not until your fundamentals are automatic. Solver output assumes you already think in ranges and frequencies. Most home-game and low-stakes players gain far more from position, hand selection, pot odds and tilt control. GTO study is the step after those.
How long does it take to get better at poker?
A single leak, such as playing too many hands, can be fixed in two weeks of daily practice. Instincts you can trust under pressure take a few hundred decisions with feedback. Years at the table without feedback do not count; players repeat the same mistake for a decade.
